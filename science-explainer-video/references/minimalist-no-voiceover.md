## Text takes over the explanatory role

Rewrite the script for reading; do not merely mute an existing narrated video or paste its long narration into captions. Put the needed causal statement directly beside the visible change.

- Prefer one short sentence per beat, usually one or two lines. A compact line of about 12–24 Chinese characters is a useful starting point, not a hard rule. Break the reasoning into more beats when necessary.
- Show the phenomenon, change one condition, reveal its effect, then show the concise conclusion. Ask a concrete question before introducing terminology or a formula.
- When a formula is necessary, introduce its quantities visually first and highlight the matching term and object together. State the condition that makes the relationship valid. Do not require a spoken explanation to complete the meaning.
- Write numerical quantities and units consistently, such as `1000 r/min`, `8.66 V`, `30°`, and `15.47%`. Use arrows, positions, labels, and units to make direction and scale understandable without sound.
- Prefer “我们” for shared reasoning when a subject is needed. Avoid repetitive first-person phrasing and unnecessary production-status labels.
- A concise title may create curiosity, but scientific scope and conclusions must remain supported by evidence. Do not carry a metaphor or personal-life analogy beyond what the underlying model supports.

## Timing and transitions

Build the timeline from **reading → visible action → observation → conclusion**, without a TTS dependency. Separate the entrance of new text from a demanding graphic change so the viewer does not have to read and interpret several new things at once.

- When using both upper and lower explanatory text, show the upper line first, hold until it can be read, then reveal the lower line while keeping the upper line visible and stable. Do not introduce or change both bands at once; simultaneous visibility after staggered entrances is intended. Apply the same order to within-scene text changes; follow [the detailed text timing rules](dynamic-tech-style.md).
- Hold short lines long enough to read comfortably once; about 3–5 seconds is an initial check, with more time for a formula or unfamiliar concept. Add observation time after the main operation. These are production starting points, not measurements taken from the reference.
- Animate meaningful changes: grow the same bars, move the marker, update the same curve, or carry the same object into the next view. Stop movement where the viewer needs to compare. Use deliberate pacing rather than a repeated decorative loop.
- **转场和文字出现不能太生硬，要丝滑。** Follow the newer reference's object continuity, reframing, draw-on paths, scans, and staged reveals. Do not reduce every scene to the same graphic dissolve and 8–16 pixel text fade; use a dissolve when object continuity does not fit.
- Separate graphics from text. Clear an outgoing sentence before a different one occupies its place. A key term may move from its source object into a formula or heading; a question may reveal phrase by phrase with a restrained cursor/mask. Reveal incoming reading text smoothly and leave the whole sentence readable. Do not crossfade complete competing text layers. Keep labels synchronized with their objects.
- Choose timing by the actual operation. Starting points include 0.6–1.2 seconds for a shared-object move/reframe, 0.4–0.8 seconds for a short phrase reveal, and 2–6 seconds for a meaningful construction/scan before a settled hold. These are original implementation suggestions, not measured reference timings. A short 8–16 pixel move/fade remains useful for quiet captions; it is not a universal entrance. Avoid bounce, arbitrary flying text, and shortening the reading hold to create speed.
- Apply the same smooth treatment to within-scene sentence changes, not just chapter boundaries. Animate presentation opacity/position rather than silently smoothing the underlying scientific data. Give a major conclusion a quiet hold.
- Review actual encoded playback and a frame sequence before, during, and after the change, including text opacity/position through the entrance and exit. Check that the intended easing is gradual, no sentence jumps in at full opacity, and all movement settles before the reading hold. Inspect several frames after completion. Explicitly check “转场后是否重影”, including stale labels, doubled curves, old sources, and alpha trails. Existing cover and transition checks remain mandatory.

Align major changes to the actual selected recording where useful, without shortening reading/observation holds. Keep the finished duration within 180–240 seconds. Extend a shorter track with a clean musical edit or crossfade, avoid abrupt loop clicks, and fade the opening/ending naturally.
